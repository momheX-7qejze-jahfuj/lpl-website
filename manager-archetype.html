The Four Archetypes

Pressure doesn't make you a worse leader. It makes you a predictable one. These are the four patterns it creates, and every one of them costs you gross, retention, and trust.

Former Star Performer

Promoted for doing the work, so under pressure they take the keys back and do it themselves. The team learns one lesson: wait for rescue.

Ivory Tower Manager

Numbers feel safe. People feel risky. This leader manages the report instead of the room, and the culture cools while the dashboards shine.

Motivator

Runs on energy instead of standards. The team rides the leader's mood, and when the leader has a bad week, the whole operation does.

Integrated Leader

The destination. Coaches behavior instead of managing numbers, sets standards that hold when moods don't, and builds a team that runs without rescue.

A Note Before You Start

From the opening page of the workbook.

We believe people already carry more capability than they can consistently access. Pressure is what quietly interrupts that access, long before it ever shows up on a scoreboard.

None of these patterns make you a bad leader. They make you a human being who learned to lead the way you were led. The only mistake is staying there once you can see it.

This workbook will not fix your shop by itself. No workbook does. But it will show you, in twenty minutes, the one pattern quietly running your leadership right now. What you do with that is the work.

— Coach Joe Clementi, Founder, The Leadership Pressure Lab
