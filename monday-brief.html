Awe Expands Perspective. Commitment Expands Possibility.

The two forces that change what a leader can see.

Coach Joe  ·  Monday, June 22, 2026  ·  3 min read

This Week · The Pressure Line

You’re Stuck on a Rock, Not a Fish

I am the furthest thing from a fisherman. I do not even like to touch them. But a professional bass fisherman, a snagged line, and one calm twist of the wrist taught me more about leading under pressure than a decade of meetings ever did.

Stand under a sky thick with stars. Stand at the edge of a canyon that took six million years to open. Stand in the room when someone does the thing they have spent a lifetime learning to do.

Something in you goes quiet.

Psychologists call it awe.

The Science of a Quiet Mind

Dacher Keltner and Jonathan Haidt gave awe its modern definition. It is what we feel in the presence of something vast, something so far past our current understanding that the mind has to stretch to hold it. They named that stretch a need for accommodation. The map you carry suddenly feels too small for the territory.

In that moment, the noise drops. Overthinking quiets. Self-criticism softens. The grip of stress loosens. You stop standing at the center of your own story. Your problems shrink. Your perspective widens.

This is more than a good feeling. A Stanford study found that awe changes how we experience time. People who felt it believed they had more of it, and that one shift made them more patient, more generous, and more willing to help. A Berkeley study found that awe quiets the ego and produces what the researchers called a small self, the version of you that is less defended and more useful to everyone around you.

The mind reboots. Energy, creativity, and insight come back online.

There is science behind this. There is also wisdom older than the science.

The Truth on the Mountain

In 1951, the Scottish mountaineer W.H. Murray wrote a passage that has outlived his expedition:

“Until one is committed, there is hesitancy, the chance to draw back. Concerning all acts of initiative (and creation), there is one elementary truth, the ignorance of which kills countless ideas and splendid plans: that the moment one definitely commits oneself, then Providence moves too.”

W.H. Murray, The Scottish Himalayan Expedition, 1951

Murray’s point is simple. Commitment changes perception. Step off the edge, and you begin to see resources, relationships, and solutions that were invisible a moment before. They did not appear. You did. Commitment changed what you were able to see.

Awe expands perspective.
Commitment expands possibility.
Both change the same thing. Not your circumstances. Your sight.

Three Moves for the Week Ahead

  1. Go looking for awe. Do not wait for it to find you. Put yourself in front of something vast this week. A night sky. A piece of music. A person at the top of their craft. Awe is not a luxury for the calendar’s good weeks. It is how a tired mind comes back online.

  2. Commit before you feel ready. Name the one decision you keep circling. The hire. The hard conversation. The standard you keep meaning to raise. Step toward it. Murray’s law holds in a dealership and on a mountain. You move first. The way forward shows up second.

  3. See what your own eye skips. You cannot proofread your own blind spots, in your writing or in your leadership. Before you send the next message that matters, run it through the Pressure Test. Let it show you the long sentence, the buried point, the line that almost lands. Then lead from clearer ground.

Reflection

Where are you standing at the edge right now, waiting to feel ready, when commitment is the only thing that will let you see the way across?

You are holding the tool. The Pressure Test on the right is built to show you what you cannot see in your own words. Run one message through it this week. Commit to being understood clearly. That is where the work starts.

The world is larger, more supportive, and more full of opportunity than you believe.

Commit first. The seeing follows.
